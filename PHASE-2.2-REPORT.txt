================================================================================
RAPIDKERT — PHASE 2.2
ONE CONTINUOUS WORLD
Homepage choreography, transitions and the project experience
================================================================================

Scope respected: the Living Ground was not rebuilt, no second WebGL scene was
added, no new media entered the library, no cards, no glassmorphism, no stock
imagery, and the commercial content is unchanged except where a section's
presentation demanded a different arrangement of the same sentences.

Files touched: index.html, rk.css, rk.js, rk-ground.js.

New review tooling, in the spirit of the existing ?scene=:

    ?stage=fld:.30              freeze one DOM stage at one point of its run
    ?stage=lyr:.5,asm:.8        several at once
    window.RK_STAGE.set(k, q)   live, from the console — k is
                                brg | fld | lyr | asm | prf
    window.RK_STAGE.free()      hand them all back to the scroll

Dead code without the parameter. It exists because a choreographed stage can
only be judged at a KNOWN point in its run, and reproducing that point by
scrolling is neither exact nor repeatable.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0 — THE ONE THING THAT MATTERED MOST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The REALITY handover was never actually seamless. It LOOKED seamless inside
the Living Ground, and then the Living Ground's section ended and the next
one began underneath it — so the visitor saw the bottom of the photograph
followed immediately by the top of the photograph again. A repeat. That was
the hardest cut on the page and it was sitting on the hinge of the whole
piece.

It is now three separate fixes stacked:

  1. THE OVERLAP. The projects section is pulled up by exactly one viewport
     (.fld { margin-top: -100svh }), so its first frame is pinned at the top
     of the screen at the same instant the Living Ground's sticky stage lets
     go. For one viewport of scroll the two are the same photograph, at the
     same crop, the same size and the same grade.

  2. THE OVERLAY MATCH. Two identical pictures only look identical if what is
     laid over them is identical too. The Living Ground's frame carried a
     vertical vignette on the photo, a vertical scrim on the stage and a
     bottom band; read against a copy offset by four hundred pixels, every
     one of those was a horizontal step across the picture. A new track,
     T.out, resolves all three to a FLAT rgba(11,12,9,.34) over the last
     chapter — and the plate underneath opens on exactly that value.

  3. THE SWAP. Matching the tone was not enough: two copies of one picture,
     offset, still show the picture starting again. So at the one frame where
     the two rectangles coincide exactly, rk-ground.js sets .is-handed and
     this frame stops drawing its own copy. Same pixels, same rectangle,
     nothing to see. What keeps travelling up is the REALITY copy — which is
     the thing that should leave.

Verified: at the join the photograph is now one continuous image across the
whole viewport, with only the REALITY chapter's own left-biased copy scrim
travelling up over it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3 — HOW PROJECTS WAS REBUILT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The old section was a staggered twelve-column photo grid: four figures, up to
150px of dead margin above each, and a near-invisible mega headline over an
empty black half-page. It is gone.

THE BRIDGE (§32.1). The photograph the model resolved into is the first
element of the section, and its edges close into a sheet while the metadata
writes itself into the space that opens. The contraction is a clip-path, so
the picture is genuinely being CROPPED into a plate rather than replaced by a
smaller copy of itself.

THE FIELD (§32.2). Seven sheets hung at authored positions on a receding
diagonal, in real CSS perspective. ONE transform on ONE element moves the
whole field laterally (260vw) and forward (8000px) through them; the plates
carry only their own depth. So travelling the entire section costs a single
transform write and seven opacity writes per frame — which is the reason this
is DOM and not a second WebGL scene: at this fidelity a canvas would buy
nothing and cost a permanent render loop.

The forward travel is deliberately large. At the first value I tried the
stations sat a few hundred pixels apart in depth, five plates were within a
stop of each other's size, and the field read as OVERLAP rather than as
depth — which is the exact failure a masonry grid has. At 8000 a plate grows
nearly four times across its approach and what is near is never in question.

Rhythm, as authored: 01 the bridge, full bleed closing to a plate; 02 narrow
and far; 03/04 a pair, one large and near, one small and deep; 05 the single
landscape interruption at 78vw; 06 a large close crop; 07/08 an architectural
pair, the last of which is crushed into the rule that enters SERVICES.

Restraint: captions carry a category and a place and nothing else, the sheet
number lives on the rig rather than being printed twice, and nothing is
claimed about any project that is not visible in the frame.

Photography treatment: the site's standard plate wash was authored to hold
type on a light page and took every frame to mud on this near-black field.
The darkening is roughly halved, and the two plates the run actually stops on
get a flatter grade still — a composition needs one frame that is simply a
good photograph.

4 — HOW SERVICES INHERIT THE LIVING GROUND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Not a three-row accordion any more. One service owns the viewport at a time
at display scale, and the outgoing state is PUSHED off by the incoming one —
the type is the transition.

Each state carries a drawing derived directly from the model:

  01 KERTÉPÍTÉS            a survey contour set, with the grade line in moss
                           and clay levelling pins
  02 ÖNTÖZŐRENDSZER        a fragment of the irrigation network in the
                           Living Ground's own vocabulary: dashed water main,
                           laterals dropping to emitters, and one charge
                           travelling the line
  03 FELSZÍN ALATTI        a CUT — two rules separating under the typography,
                           opening a root zone with driplines and wetted
                           lenses underneath

And the third state takes the whole page under grade with it: the limestone
fill cross-fades to soil and the entire type register inverts. That colour
change is scheduled into the gap between states 02 and 03, when every line on
screen is already at its lowest opacity — so it is something the visitor
feels rather than something they watch happen to a paragraph they are reading.

A narrow field plate sits at the outer edge of each state with only the tail
of the name crossing it. Below grade it carries its own scrim, because cream
type over a phone photograph of a bright driveway is not type.

Nothing was cut from the copy: the three links, the three names, the three
taglines and the three descriptions are all still real, indexable anchor text.

5 — HOW PROCESS RECONSTRUCTS THE GARDEN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

One horizontal technical datum across the stage with the five stations set on
it and a clay head travelling between them. Underneath, a section drawing that
GAINS a component per station and never replaces one:

  01 FELMÉRÉS             the site line alone
  02 TERV ÉS AJÁNLAT      dimension ticks, a level line, an overall run
  03 TEREP ÉS TALAJMUNKA  the ground section — three strata, cut edges, grit
  04 KIVITELEZÉS          the irrigation main, emitters and root systems
  05 ÁTADÁS               the finished surface: turf line, blades, two slabs

Because every group that has been reached stays on the drawing, the section
is assembled rather than switched — which is the difference between an
assembly and five slides.

The section also comes back up into the light as it completes: it starts on
the soil the third service left the page in and crosses to limestone as the
finished surface goes on, scheduled into the gap between stations 04 and 05.
The colour is telling the same story as the drawing.

Layout: title left, current stage right, datum across both, drawing beneath.
Stacking the written stage under the title put a 3rem h3 and a 3rem h2 in the
same 200px of an 800px stage and they collided outright.

On a phone the datum runs VERTICALLY down the gutter with the stations on it
and the drawing to its right — re-choreographed, not rotated.

6 — HOW THE CTA CLOSES THE OPENING SURFACE CONCEPT

The page opens with "A jó kert nem a felszínen kezdődik." and the visitor has

One rule across the page in moss, with grass ticks on it, a ±0,00 FELSZÍN

change between that block and the footer — the footer is the last stratum of
the same composition.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8 — HOW LAND -> LIFE PACING CHANGED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SCROLL TIME IS NOT SCENE TIME.

The camera keys, every track, every chapter's data-from and the eleven
reduced-motion stops are all authored in SCENE time, and that authoring is
correct. What was wrong was the SHARE OF SCROLL each scene segment was given:
LAND -> LIFE ran five complete transformations of the object in 0.028 of the
timeline each, while six quiet beats between chapters — where nothing happens
but a camera move — held 0.181 between them, and the REALITY handover, the
hinge of the page, had 0.056.

So Phase 2.2 adds a monotone map from scroll to scene (WARP, rk-ground.js
§08.5) and NOTHING else moves. Not one tuned stop value was retimed.

                          share of scroll
                        BEFORE      NOW
    six quiet beats     .181        .096      (-47%)
    DESCENT             .084        .060
    GROUND              .156        .130
    WATER               .160        .140
    STRUCTURE           .118        .100
    TEREP               .030        .052      (+73%)
    VÍZ                 .028        .050      (+79%)
    STRUKTÚRA           .026        .048      (+85%)
    NÖVÉNYZET           .026        .048      (+85%)
    KÉSZ KERT           .030        .052      (+73%)
    REALITY             .056        .124      (+121%)

The five construction steps go from ~26svh of scroll each to ~44svh, and the
handover from 48svh to 107svh. The page did not get longer to pay for it.

Fritsch-Carlson monotone cubic rather than straight segments: a piecewise
linear map has a velocity step at every knot, and a velocity step in the
middle of a continuous camera move reads as a gear change. The limiter is
what guarantees the curve cannot overshoot and briefly run the scene
BACKWARDS, which is the one failure mode this map must not have.

Damping stays on the SCROLL side. Damp in scene time and a stretched segment
would also become a sluggish one, which is the opposite of what stretching it
was for.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9 — HOW THE WATER COMPOSITION WAS FIXED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two changes, both compositional, plus one that was a straight bug.

THE CAMERA. Pulled back about 18%, because the block bled off three edges and
there was no silhouette to read the trench against; and panned harder left,
which parks the excavation — the labelled emitter, the lateral running out of
it, and the cut wall 0.20m from the x=1.15 dripline — inside the left two
thirds, and hands the right third to the copy column instead of making the
copy sit on soil. The body copy no longer overlaps either the model or the
leader lines.

THE LABELS. Three became two: "Fővezeték" sat furthest from the emitter and
pulled the eye to the opposite side of the frame from the evidence it was
meant to introduce. Both survivors now lead LEFT, away from the copy.

THE BAND. This was the bug. The reach was 1.12, which — measured through the
0.58 lateral squash and the 1.05 vertical one — put the wetting front 1.93m
either side of a dripline and 1.07m above and below it. The laterals are
2.30m apart and the root zone is 1.42m thick. So the bands MET each other
sideways and OVERRAN the stratum vertically: the whole layer went dark to its
own edges, which is exactly the failure the code's own comment warns about.
The eye read "this stratum is a different soil", not "water is spreading".

Reach is now 0.52: ±0.90m sideways, ±0.50m vertically, leaving 0.51m of dry
earth between adjacent driplines and 0.21m above and below inside the root
zone. The shape only exists if there is dry earth on the other side of it.
Because the band is a third the size it was, the contrast per square metre
went up to pay for it — albedo and roughness only, no emissive term, no blue,
nothing added to the frame that is not soil.

Measured, by differencing the rendered frame at uMoist 0 against uMoist 1:
the effect is confined to the middle band of the frame and the left 45% of
its width — the same third of the frame the leader lines point into. Cause
and evidence are now in one glance.

HONEST LIMIT: it is a quiet effect. Mean per-pixel difference across the whole
frame is 1.7/765; inside the affected region it is 15-19. Stronger than
before, correctly shaped, and still the thing I would look at next.

11 — ANNOTATION REDUCTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Also a bug, and a bad one. updateAnnotations() writes opacity as an INLINE
style, which outranks the stylesheet's opacity:0 — so a label that stopped
being current was never actually zeroed. The only thing hiding it was that a
slow scroll passes through the fade band and lands near zero on its own.
Scroll fast, restore a scroll position, or jump the timeline, and GROUND's
five strata, WATER's pipes and EXPLODED's six letters were on screen at once,
over each other and over the headline. Fixed.

Then the deliberate cuts:

  SURFACE     unchanged — the horizon and its two marks
  GROUND      five strata labels kept, because the strata ARE the chapter.
              Three second lines dropped ("gyep · burkolat", "drén",
              "teherhordó") — a fourth level of information competing with
              the leader lines at the moment the visitor is counting layers.
  WATER       three labels -> two
  EXPLODED    thirteen live elements -> eight. The five stratum letters that
              rode the dimension string are gone: they said A B C D E and the
              labels beside them say "A Felszín", "B Termőtalaj" thirty
              millimetres away. Mobile had already dropped them for exactly
              that reason. The rule, its closed ends, the ticks, the datum
              and the caption are what make it read as a measured drawing —
              the lettering never was.

Roughly a 38% reduction in live annotation elements, and the part that went
was duplication, not information.

12 — TYPOGRAPHIC AND TONAL RHYTHM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

TYPE. Before, every section headline was --t-mega and every section looked
like the same section. There are now four registers and they are enforced:

  MONUMENTAL   four places only — the hero, the three service names, the five
               proof statements, the closing line
  SECTION      the projects, process and FAQ titles, at roughly a third of
               that. A section title is not a spectacle.
  TECHNICAL    mono, --t-label — plates, rig, stations, captions, datum marks
  EDITORIAL    quiet running copy, deliberately small and unemphatic

COLOUR. The page was black / cream / black / cream with the change at every
section edge. It now moves

  deep soil -> warm charcoal -> limestone -> back under grade -> up into
  limestone -> paper -> limestone -> soil

and — the part that matters — three of those changes happen DURING a
transition rather than at a boundary: the field warms across its run, the
services cross grade between states 02 and 03, and the assembly comes back
into the light between stations 04 and 05.

HEADER. A limestone slab with a filled cream button on it, over a cinematic
sequence, is a website bar over a film. Over the Living Ground and the project
field the bar now integrates: no sheet, no fill, the call to action becomes a
hairline outline, and the whole bar rests at 58% presence — returning to full
the instant it is pointed at, focused, or the menu is opened. Navigation is
never removed and never unreachable.

Below that it reads the tone of whatever is actually under it, including the
two stages that rewrite their own tone as they cross grade. Measured over a
full scroll: five clean states, no flicker. "Solid after the hero" stopped
describing this page.

POINTER. A NÉZET label over the project field only. Everywhere else the
cursor is native — a bespoke cursor on every page is a costume.

16 — REMAINING WEAK TRANSITION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROJECTS -> SERVICES.

The mechanism is right and it is visible: the last plate is crushed into a
hairline and that hairline runs full bleed as the section ends. But the rule
it becomes sits at 42% of the field's stage, and the rule it hands to — the
divider under the services header — sits at about 17% of the next one. A rule
survives the boundary, which is more than any other boundary on the old page
managed, but it does not land in the same place, so the eye reads "a rule,
then a rule" rather than "that rule".

Making them land together means either dropping the services header to
mid-screen, which wrecks that section's composition, or lifting the field's
whole horizon, which pushes the plates off the top. It needs one of the two
sections re-laid out around the join, and I stopped rather than damage a
composition that works to improve a join that is merely good.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
17 — THE SECTION I WOULD STILL CALL LEAST AWWWARDS-READY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROOF.

It does exactly what §5 of the brief asked — one claim at a time, monumental,
with real emptiness — and it is a genuine improvement on a four-row stat
table. But it is the one section on the page whose idea is entirely
TYPOGRAPHIC. Everything else earns its screen with an object: the model, the
photograph, the field, the drawings, the assembly, the grade line. Proof has
a number, a rule and white space, and if you took the number away there would
be nothing there.

It also carries the least of the page's own vocabulary: SURFACE, CUT, DEPTH
and ASSEMBLY are all absent from it. The rule under each statement is called
a grade line in the stylesheet, and it is the only thing tying it back.

What it wants is one restrained graphic idea of its own — the 40 km as an
actual radius, the 70% as two soil columns rather than a percentage — built
in the same drawing language as everything else. That is a design decision I
did not want to make unilaterally with invented geometry, and it is the first
thing I would put in front of the client.

Runner-up: the project field's closing beat. Plates 07 and 08 arrive and one
of them is immediately crushed, so the last thing the visitor sees of the
work is a plate leaving rather than a plate arriving. The pair reads, but it
resolves rather than lands.


================================================================================
18 — CONTRACTS THAT ARE SILENT WHEN BROKEN
================================================================================

The two from Phase 2.1 still hold — THE HERO DATUM and THE PAVED FOOTPRINT.
Phase 2.2 adds three:

1. THE PACING MAP.
   WARP in rk-ground.js §08.5 maps SCROLL time to SCENE time. Every camera
   key, every track, every data-from in index.html and every SNAP stop is in
   SCENE time and must stay there. Retiming a chapter means editing its
   data-from AND its track stops, exactly as before; changing how much SCROLL
   a chapter gets means editing WARP and nothing else. Confusing the two
   silently desynchronises the copy from the scene.

2. THE HANDOVER TRIO.
   .fld { margin-top: -100svh } in rk.css, the one-viewport hold in rk.js
   §12.1, and .gd.is-handed set from rk-ground.js are ONE mechanism. The
   overlap is what makes the join invisible, the hold is what stops the
   bridge's contraction happening where nobody can see it, and the swap is
   what stops the picture visibly restarting. Remove any one and the seam
   comes back — and it comes back looking like a bug in the other two.
   The bridge plate's filter must also stay byte-identical to --grade, which
   is what .gd__photo img computes to at --ph:1.

3. THE PRESENCE WIDTH.
   presence(q, i, n, width) in rk.js. A slot's window reaches zero at
   |u| = 1/width and adjacent centres are 1 apart in u, so 2.0 is the one
   value at which the outgoing state hits zero exactly as the incoming one
   leaves it. Services and assembly are both 2.0 for that reason. Wider and
   there are stretches with nothing on screen; narrower and two states are
   legible on top of each other at the same origin. Proof is deliberately 2.6
   — there the emptiness is the argument.

Debug: ?scene= and ?tier= as before, plus ?stage=<brg|fld|lyr|asm|prf>:<q>
and window.RK_STAGE.
