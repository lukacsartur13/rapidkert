0 — THE ONE THING THAT MATTERED MOST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The single highest-leverage find was a BUG, not a taste call.

The grass blades carried a skyward shading normal but were drawn double-sided,
so for every back-facing blade Three.js flipped that normal to point at the
ground — where the only light is the hemisphere's dark soil term. Roughly half
the sward rendered black. That is why the lawn read as "coarse stippling" at
every range, in every chapter.

Cancelling the flip is one multiply. Combined with the new shell sward it fixed
the hero, KÉSZ KERT and the handoff frame at once.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1 — HERO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

COMPOSITION
Rebuilt, not tuned. An empty element inside the h1 opens a measured band
between "NEM A FELSZÍNEN" and "KEZDŐDIK.", and the camera is SOLVED every frame
so the specimen's grade plane projects onto exactly that band. Two headline
lines sit above ground, one below, and the rule between them is the model's
actual surface. Type and object no longer share a rectangle — they share one
horizontal system.

MATERIALS
The lawn holds at hero scale: mown bands, clumping, contact fringes at the
paving. Stepping stones read as cut stone — chamfered arris, embedded body,
per-slab grain and dimensions.

LIGHTING
Unchanged and still correct: warm key, plus the frontal section fill that keeps
the near-vertical strata legible.

TYPOGRAPHY RELATIONSHIP
The former collision is gone — the FELSZÍN marker used to sit inside the word
FELSZÍNEN. Both horizon markers now sit below the rule, in the band the datum
opens. The scrim dropped from a full-height 90% wash across the left half to a
single soft pool under the lower-left copy, roughly a third of its former
strength. Nothing in the hero overlaps the specimen any more.

REMAINING WEAKNESS
At exactly 1280x800 the "MEGÉPÍTETT KERTEK" button's right border crosses about
ten pixels onto the block's near corner. It survives, but it is tolerated, not
designed. The lede column and the block edge are within ~60px at that width and
the composition has no slack there.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2 — GROUND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

COMPOSITION
This was the worst frame in the piece: the block bled off all four edges, so
there was no silhouette and no obvious subject inside a wall of soil. The camera
is now pulled back far enough to close the object's outline and pushed right so
the copy owns a real column instead of a scrim over the model.

MATERIAL READABILITY
Five strata separate cleanly. The drainage course is the strongest read; the
sub-base now carries compression lifts, two aggregate grades and occasional
oversize inclusions without becoming loud enough to compete with the root zone.

ROOTS
Improved, with real hierarchy: per-system vigour, and a third order of fine
roots at a third the diameter of the laterals. Roots are now clipped by the
excavation volume, so they end flush with the cut walls and stand proud of them
by about 22cm — severed roots in a trench wall, rather than the metre of pale
spaghetti hanging in open air that seeding them near the trench used to produce.

REMAINING WEAKNESS
Honest assessment: roots still barely register in GROUND. They live inside
opaque soil, so the only place they can be seen is the trench, and from this
camera that is a small part of the frame. They read WELL in EXPLODED and
ADEQUATELY in WATER. Making them genuinely strong in GROUND would require
drawing root cross-sections into the cut faces themselves — a new system, and
out of scope for this phase.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3 — WATER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PIPE READABILITY
Good. The polymer stays matte and dark. Two new lights do the work: a low
bounce sitting near the trench floor that lifts the base of the cut walls, and
a narrow warm source above the excavation's inside corner that separates the
cut edge — the line that says "this ground has been opened" — from the wall
behind it. The ambient was not raised; the light was shaped.

PULSE
Rebuilt. The head is roughly five times wider and about 40% dimmer, with a soft
leading edge and a much shorter luminance range. The colour lost nearly all
remaining chroma, because additive blending compounds any saturation into the
neon worm the brief asked to remove. The pipe's own wall now lifts slightly
from the inside as the charge passes, which is what stops the water reading as
a light travelling ALONGSIDE a pipe rather than pressure travelling THROUGH one.

Timing was also wrong and is fixed: the soil used to finish wetting BEFORE the
water reached the emitters. Cause now precedes effect, and the bulb opens over
roughly a second of reading time.

MOISTURE
The real fix was structural, not cosmetic. Point bulbs around the six emitters
were physically reasonable and compositionally useless: the emitters hang in
the OPEN TRENCH, where the soil that would show the wetting has been dug away.
The only evidence was a partial disc on a wall a metre and a half behind them.

Wetting is now measured from the lateral's AXIS — a continuous band, which is
what subsurface drip actually produces — and is deliberately SHORTER than the
root zone is thick, so dry earth remains above and below it and the shape
between them reads as water spreading. The boundary is warped by noise so it is
never an ellipse. Half the read comes from roughness rather than albedo: wet
soil is smoother, so the wetted field separates under any light instead of only
a bright one. No droplets, no blue, no glow.

LIGHTING
The underground stays darker than the surface. Both new lights are off above
grade and off again once the section comes apart.

REMAINING WEAKNESS
The wetted lenses read most strongly on the far-left cut face, while the labels
point at the emitter on the right. Cause and evidence are in different parts of
the frame.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4 — EXPLODED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LAYER HIERARCHY
Vertical steps are now unequal and each stratum drifts a few centimetres
sideways — an assembly eased apart by hand rather than a stack of cards. The
drift is under 3% of the block's width, so the object still plainly aligns on
one vertical axis. The irrigation network was resting ON the root slab's top
face; it now separates into its own open gap and reads as a part in its own
right.

TECHNICAL DRAWING QUALITY
A dimension string with closed end ticks down the left of the object, a tick to
every stratum boundary, stratum letters, a ±0,00 FELSZÍN datum drawn at TRUE
grade, and a caption. Everything is projected from the real layer groups, so
when the section eases apart the drawing eases apart with it.

The marks carry LETTERS, NOT DEPTHS. A figure such as "−0,45" would read as a
Rapidkert construction specification; this is a diagram of an order of layers,
and the caption says so in as many words ("Elvi rétegrend — séma, nem
kivitelezési méret"). ±0,00 is the only figure used, and it is definitionally
the surface rather than a fabricated dimension.

DEPTH
The fix that mattered most here: every stratum's soffit faces straight down, and
in a scene lit entirely from above that meant five black rectangles the moment
the section separated. The parts had no thickness because you could not see the
bottom of any of them. A bounce light tied directly to the explode value gives
them solidity. The camera also now sits ABOVE the top slab — it was below it,
which is precisely why the lawn was the darkest layer in the drawing.

REMAINING WEAKNESS
The body copy's last word sits about fifteen pixels from the dimension string's
"D" tick. It clears, but only just, and a longer translation of that paragraph
would collide.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5 — FINISHED (KÉSZ KERT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SURFACE QUALITY
Transformed. The sward is now built the way fur is: ten shells lifted off the
surface along its normal, each discarding every fragment not inside a blade's
cross-section at that height, over two lattices at unrelated scales so the
sward never resolves into the grid it is built on. About 34k triangles for a
density no instanced field could reach, in ONE draw call. A few thousand real
blades remain, weighted hard toward the block's edges, where a shell stack has
nothing left to draw.

LANDSCAPE DETAIL
Stepping stones have individual dimensions, settlement and rotation. The
terrace is a 3x2 large-format joint grid on a bedding course that oversails it
on every side. An analytic footprint SDF — one source of truth, consumed by the
geometry, by the sowing and by the shader — stops grass under the slabs, adds a
contact occlusion gradient and a thin bright lip at the joint. No SSAO pass.

LIGHTING
Warm final grade; the paving casts a real shadow onto the sward.

REMAINING WEAKNESS
The mowing stripes are almost too subtle to survive at this camera distance —
they read at the handoff frame and barely here. The lawn is also still on the
dark, olive side of where I would ideally put it. That is consistent with the
scene's palette, but it is a compromise with the dark page, not an optimum.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6 — REALITY HANDOFF
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ALIGNMENT
The biggest change in this section is that THE PHOTOGRAPH WAS WRONG.
kesz-kert-panorama.jpg is a bare-earth plot under an open sky. It matched
neither the top-down lawn it was growing out of nor the sentence above it
("Nem látványterv. Megépített kert.").

Swapped for kesz-gyep-terasz.jpg, from the same frozen library: a finished mown
lawn seen from a high oblique, paving down the right edge, a terrace plate, and
an irrigation valve cover sitting in the grass — the same objects the model
has. The final camera then moved off plan view to roughly the photographer's
own position, about forty degrees above the lawn. A plan view clipped more
neatly to the top face, but a neat clip is not the point; the point is the half
second in which the visitor cannot tell which of the two they are looking at,
and that only happens if both frames were shot from the same place.

TIMING
The plate no longer fades in — a cross-dissolve advertises that the frames
never matched. It takes the frame at near-full opacity, and the GRADE resolves
afterwards. That lag is the ambiguous moment. The render is also held crisp
underneath it much later than before, instead of dissolving out from under a
growing photo.

COLOUR MATCH
The photograph arrives desaturated, cooler and darker — inside the render's
palette — and resolves to the site's normal photo grade. Its vignette comes UP
with the grade rather than down: while the plate is pretending to be the render
it has to be as evenly lit as the render is.

The REALITY chapter's centred radial scrim had to go. Multiplied with the
photo's own vignette it took the finished garden to nearly black at the exact
moment it was supposed to be revealed. It is now left-biased and no heavier
than the hero's, and the copy column was narrowed so the photograph gets the
rest of the frame.

REMAINING WEAKNESS
The rhyme is now genuinely close, but not seamless. The photograph has a house
and a fence above the lawn where the render has empty background, so the top of
the frame gives the game away a beat earlier than the bottom does.

7 — MOBILE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

HERO
Recomposed rather than scaled. A phone has no left and right to divide, so the
headline divides vertically and the specimen drops into the gap: "A jó kert /
nem a felszínen" above the grade line, the section itself in the band below it,
"kezdődik." underneath. The object becomes punctuation inside the headline
system — the same composition as desktop, stacked.

Also fixed a real bug here: the exponential fog is a function of ABSOLUTE
distance from the camera, and portrait viewports pull the camera back by up to
2.45x to fit the block. The fog was eating half the specimen and the phone hero
rendered as a grey ghost of itself. Density is now relative to the framing
distance, which keeps the same depth cue ACROSS the object on every viewport.

UNDERGROUND
Legible. Labels reduced, subject scaled up, less elevation so the cut faces do
not foreshorten. A phone cannot hold all five strata AND the copy, so it does
not try: the surface, topsoil and root zone stay clear above the fold and the
drainage and sub-base descend into the scrim the copy sits on. The section
reads as continuing into darkness rather than being veiled.

EXPLODED
The drawing keeps its apparatus but not its duplicates. The dimension rule and
its ticks stay — they are what make the object read as a measured drawing — and
the lettering is dropped, because on a phone it repeated the stratum labels
thirty millimetres away and ran off the left edge doing it. The datum label
moves back to the dimension string, where there is clear space.

REMAINING WEAKNESS
On mobile GROUND the specimen occupies only about a third of the screen height,
with dead space above it. It follows the intended split but it is under-scaled,
and I stopped short of retuning the narrow-viewport framing constants further
because doing so would have put the newly settled mobile hero back at risk.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10 — TWO CONTRACTS THAT ARE SILENT WHEN BROKEN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Anyone editing this next needs to know:

1. THE HERO DATUM
   .gd__datum is an empty element inside the h1, between headline lines two and
   three. Its band height and its --anchor value are authored in rk.css;
   rk-ground.js reads both back and SOLVES the hero camera so the specimen's
   grade plane projects onto it. Change the headline markup, the band height or
   --anchor, and the model silently stops landing on the type. The lock
   releases by p ≈ 0.09.

2. THE PAVED FOOTPRINT
   PAVERS / TERRACE in rk-ground.js are consumed three times: by the paver
   geometry, by paveSD() when sowing the sward, and by rkPaveSD() in GLSL for
   the grass keep-out and the contact shading. All three must agree to the
   centimetre or grass grows through the slabs.

Debug: ?scene=hero|descent|ground|water|exploded|terep|viz|struktura|noveny|
kesz|photo and ?tier=low|med|high. ?scene= also exposes window.RK_GROUND —
.bare() strips DOM overlays for screenshots, .U is the live uniform table.
To hold a value for A/B comparison, override the track (e.g. T.moist = () => 0);
the p setter and .hold() are both overwritten by the next frame.
